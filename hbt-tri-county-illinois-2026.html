HBT Financial Consolidates Central Illinois

Heartland Bank's parent acquires First State Bank for $204.6 million, pushing the combined organization to $8.3 billion in assets and tightening its grip on central and northern Illinois.

By Dhiren Dommeti · August 10, 2026 · 4 min read · Banking · M&A

Illinois community banking is getting smaller by design. HBT Financial, the publicly traded Bloomington-based parent of Heartland Bank and Trust, is acquiring Tri-County Financial Group, the parent of First State Bank, in a deal valued at approximately $204.6 million. The transaction adds 19 branches across central and northern Illinois and pushes the combined organization to $8.3 billion in total assets, making it one of the larger community banking franchises in the state.

What HBT Is Acquiring

Tri-County Financial Group is the holding company for First State Bank, a community lender operating 19 branches throughout central and northern Illinois. As of June 30, 2026, First State Bank held approximately $1.6 billion in total assets, $1.3 billion in loans, and $1.3 billion in deposits. For an institution of its size, that is a substantial deposit base, and it reflects the bank's deep roots in smaller Illinois markets.

Under the merger agreement, Tri-County shareholders can elect to receive 2.4589 HBT Financial shares for each Tri-County share, $71.01 in cash per share, or a combination of both. The structure gives sellers flexibility while letting HBT manage its capital position. It's a fairly standard approach for community bank M&A where both stock and cash play roles in making the deal economics work for both sides.

Why HBT Is Buying

HBT Financial has been one of the more acquisitive community banking platforms in Illinois over the past several years, methodically expanding its central Illinois franchise through a combination of organic growth and targeted deals. The Tri-County acquisition follows that playbook: acquire a well-run institution in adjacent markets, integrate operations onto HBT's platform, and extract efficiency savings that neither bank could achieve alone.

First State Bank operates in communities where Heartland Bank has either no presence or limited coverage. The combined network spans central and northern Illinois as well as eastern Iowa and suburban St. Louis, a regional footprint that would have taken years to build organically.

"This combination strengthens our position in markets we know well and adds communities where First State Bank has built lasting relationships."
— HBT Financial management, per merger announcement, August 2026

The $10 Billion Question

At $8.3 billion in pro forma assets, HBT Financial is creeping up on the $10 billion regulatory threshold that triggers a more intensive supervisory framework under the Dodd-Frank Act. Banks that cross that line face Durbin Amendment interchange fee restrictions, enhanced consumer financial protection oversight, and stress testing requirements that can meaningfully dent profitability.

HBT's management has been deliberate about pacing its acquisitions with that threshold in mind. The Tri-County deal puts the bank close enough that one more sizable acquisition would push it over. The market will be watching whether HBT pauses to absorb the integration or keeps acquiring, knowing that crossing $10 billion would be the next major strategic shift rather than something to sidestep.

What to Watch · Key Takeaways

  • At $8.3 billion pro forma, HBT is within striking distance of the $10 billion Dodd-Frank threshold — the next acquisition could be the one that triggers a meaningfully different regulatory and profitability environment.
  • The deal expected to close Q1 2027, giving HBT a full integration cycle before year-end 2027 reporting — watch for margin improvement commentary as the efficiency savings are realized.
  • First State Bank's deposit base is heavily concentrated in smaller Illinois markets — sticky, low-cost funding that should improve HBT's overall cost of deposits.
  • Illinois community banking consolidation is accelerating. This is one of multiple deals announced in the state in 2026 alone — expect the number of independent Illinois community banks to keep declining.
