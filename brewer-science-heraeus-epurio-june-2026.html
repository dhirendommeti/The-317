Brewer Science Makes Vertical Integration Bet

Brewer Science is acquiring Heraeus Epurio's semiconductor chemicals business to gain control over critical materials, strengthening its position in an increasingly strategic supply chain — and highlighting what manufacturers are starting to prioritize.

By Ashrith Desu · June 18, 2026 · 5 min read · Industrials · M&A

Most acquisitions move a company sideways or upward in its market. This one moves Brewer Science down — deeper into the supply chain it depends on.

Brewer Science, the Rolla, Missouri-based maker of specialty materials for semiconductor manufacturing, announced June 18 it will acquire the semiconductor chemicals business of Heraeus Epurio. The deal includes a manufacturing facility in Dayton, Ohio, along with sales and support operations across Asia. Financial terms were not disclosed.

What Brewer is buying isn't a new product line bolted onto its existing business. It's direct ownership of the ultrapure specialty chemicals that go into the materials it already sells.

Deal Overview

Brewer Science has spent decades building products that semiconductor manufacturers rely on for advanced chip production. Until now, the specialty chemicals underlying those products — the raw materials — have come largely from outside suppliers. This acquisition changes that. By taking over Heraeus Epurio's semiconductor chemicals business, Brewer gains direct ownership of the manufacturing capability for the materials that go into its own products, rather than depending on a third party for them.

What Brewer Actually Bought

Semiconductor manufacturing runs on extraordinarily tight purity requirements. A trace contaminant invisible to the naked eye can ruin a chip. Heraeus Epurio's business produces exactly the kind of ultrapure specialty chemicals that meet those standards: monomers, crosslinkers, photo-initiators, and photoacid generators — inputs used in the photoresist and materials chemistry that pattern modern chips.

That's a different layer of the supply chain than the one Brewer has historically occupied. The acquisition moves the company upstream, from formulating finished materials to owning the specialty chemicals manufacturing that feeds them.

"As materials are becoming increasingly critical to the advanced semiconductor value chain worldwide, this vertical integration further strengthens our ability to deliver superior quality and a reliable, timely global supply of materials solutions, enabling us to consistently exceed our customers' expectations."
— Dr. Sri Kommu, Co-CEO, Brewer Science

Why This Deal Matters

The semiconductor industry has spent the last several years relearning a lesson about supply chains: control over critical materials is not a back-office concern, it's a competitive one. Chipmakers that depend on a thin set of suppliers for specialty chemicals have been exposed to shortages, lead-time spikes, and qualification bottlenecks whenever that supply gets disrupted.

By acquiring Heraeus Epurio's business outright, Brewer reduces its own dependence on third parties for materials it considers essential to its product line. It also buys something that's much harder to replicate quickly: an established, qualified manufacturing operation. Building ultrapure specialty chemicals capability from scratch, and getting it qualified by semiconductor customers, takes months or years. Acquiring a business that already clears that bar compresses that timeline to the moment the deal closes.

What to Watch · Key Takeaways

  • Financial terms have not been disclosed. Brewer Science is privately held and employee-owned, so there is no public filing requirement that would force that information into view.
  • The Dayton, Ohio facility and Asia-based sales and support operations transfer as part of the deal — giving Brewer a direct manufacturing and customer-support footprint it did not previously operate.
  • This is a vertical integration play, not a horizontal one: Brewer isn't expanding into a new market, it's taking ownership of a layer of its own supply chain.
  • Expect more deals like this across the semiconductor materials space as manufacturers continue prioritizing supply chain control over reliance on third-party chemical suppliers.

None of this shows up on a quarterly earnings call, since Brewer doesn't have one. But the logic behind the deal is the same logic showing up across the semiconductor industry: when a material is critical enough, the safest place to make it is inside your own walls.
