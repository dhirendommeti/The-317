Brightstar Capital Expands Architecture Platform

A PE-backed architecture platform adds a healthcare and senior living specialist, as demand for aging-related infrastructure accelerates.

By Ashrith Desu · June 24, 2026 · 5 min read · Professional Services · PE Buyout

On the surface, it looks like another architecture industry roll-up. Underneath, it's a bet on demographics — and on who controls the data behind where the next generation of healthcare buildings gets built.

Brightstar Capital Partners announced its acquisition of Erdman, a Wisconsin-based architecture and engineering firm with a specialization in healthcare and senior living. The deal gives Brightstar a nationally recognized healthcare design specialist to add to the architecture and design platform it began assembling after acquiring KZF Design in 2025. Financial terms were not disclosed.

What Brightstar Actually Bought

Erdman focuses almost exclusively on healthcare and senior living, designing architecture ranging from hospitals and medical office buildings to independent living communities, assisted living facilities, and ambulatory care facilities.

Erdman also brings the technology that many architecture firms lack. The firm developed ZeroIn, a proprietary healthcare analytics platform that helps providers evaluate markets, forecast demand, and plan facility investments. That combination of design expertise, national reach, and data capability makes Erdman far more significant to the platform than a traditional design firm would be on its own.

"Erdman brings sector depth, a national footprint, and a proprietary analytics capability that we believe strengthens the platform."
— Michael Burke, Board Chair, Brightstar's Architecture & Design Platform

Why This Deal Matters

Architecture firms are increasingly valued less for drafting capability and more for industry-specific expertise. A hospital expansion project requires fluency in patient flows, clinical operations, reimbursement trends, regulatory requirements, and demographic demand — knowledge that takes decades to build. Brightstar gets immediate access to the healthcare-focused relationships, talent, and project history that Erdman has built over more than 70 years.

The deal also gives Brightstar exposure to one of the most reliable demand drivers in the economy: an aging population. The U.S. population over 80 is expected to grow substantially over the next two decades, driving demand for senior housing, assisted living facilities, and healthcare infrastructure. Erdman sits squarely inside that trend.

The Platform Strategy

The acquisition is less about Erdman as a standalone business and more about where Brightstar is taking its broader strategy. Private equity firms have spent years consolidating accounting firms, insurance brokers, and IT providers. Architecture and engineering are becoming the next frontier — an industry that remains fragmented, where hundreds of firms have the expertise but lack the scale to compete for the largest, most complex projects.

Brightstar's approach is straightforward: acquire high-quality regional firms, preserve their local relationships, share resources across the platform, and build something large enough to win bigger work. KZF Design provided the foundation; Erdman adds healthcare specialization and an immediate national footprint.

What to Watch · Key Takeaways

  • Financial terms have not been disclosed. Both Brightstar and Erdman are privately held, so there is no public filing requirement that would surface the price.
  • ZeroIn, Erdman's healthcare analytics platform, is arguably the more durable asset in the deal — it creates a recurring, data-driven relationship with providers that extends well beyond a single design project.
  • This is the second leg of Brightstar's architecture platform, following the 2025 acquisition of KZF Design — expect the roll-up to keep adding regional specialists rather than stopping here.
  • The strategic logic mirrors what private equity has already done in accounting, insurance brokerage, and IT services: consolidate a fragmented industry around shared infrastructure and data.

One key asset in the deal is ZeroIn. As providers make increasingly data-driven decisions about forecasts, investments, and expansion, that platform gives Brightstar a recurring capability that extends beyond traditional design services. It moves the firm up the value chain — from designing buildings to influencing the decisions that determine whether those buildings get built in the first place.
