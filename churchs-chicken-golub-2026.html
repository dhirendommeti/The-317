Church's Chicken's Next Chapter

Golub Capital's investment signals that Church's multi-year turnaround is complete, and they can now shift toward expansion

By Ashrith Desu · 2026 · 4 min read · Private Equity · Consumer

Church's Texas announced its growth equity investment from Golub Capital, providing capital to accelerate expansion, remodel existing locations, and continue investing in franchise growth. The deal was purposely done at this point in time, sitting as a key milestone in a successful turnaround. Church's isn't struggling, but was able to rebuild its foundation and can now prepare to scale.

"This new capital is about adding fuel at exactly the right moment. High Bluff remains as committed to Church's as the day we bought it. Golub Capital's investment reflects the progress we've made and will help further accelerate our growth plans as Church's continues on its trajectory." — Ryan Pitt, Principal, High Bluff Capital Partners

Church's operates more than 1,500 restaurants across the U.S., and in more than 25 international markets. After previously being acquired by High Bluff and Future Standard in 2021, they've undergone a huge operational transformation. They were able to enhance franchise economics by modernizing its supply chain, launching a proprietary app and loyalty program, refreshing the menu and marketing, and investing in franchise operations and profitability.

Already, the results have been meaningful. Average domestic franchise sales have increased by more than $200,000 per restaurant, generating $55,000+ in additional annual profit for franchisees. Additionally, systemwide sales crossed $1.6 billion in 2025, achieving its first year of positive net U.S. unit growth since 2010.

Why The Deal Matters

PE firms create value in two main phases.

This deal is their transition into phase two. Rather than using capital to stabilize, they are able to use it to expand, remodel, and support franchises. These few years of fixing store-level economics will help them reduce execution risk and only strengthen expansion as they go forward.

One indicator that makes this investment promising isn't just Golub's expertise, or Church's foundation. Existing lenders have proven to have a deeper understanding of a company's financial performance over new investors, and Golub has been financing them since 2024 now, on a smaller level.

When Golub also chooses to invest equity rather than debt, it signals their own confidence in Church's execution and the company's long-term growth. Sponsors are increasingly turning toward structured growth capital rather than pursuing an immediate exit.
