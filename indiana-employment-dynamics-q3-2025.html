Indiana's Labor Market Nearly Treads Water in Q3 2025 — But the Trend Is Improving

The state shed a net 664 private-sector jobs in the third quarter — a dramatic improvement over the prior quarter's 15,254-job loss, but a sign that Indiana's employment churn is running on razor-thin margins heading into year-end.

By The 317 Staff · May 18, 2026 · 6 min read · Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

Indiana's private sector generated 143,512 gross job gains in the third quarter of 2025 while shedding 144,176 — leaving the state with a net loss of just 664 jobs over the period, according to Business Employment Dynamics data released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The numbers tell a story of near-equilibrium: the state's job creation and destruction engines are running at almost identical speeds, and the margin between them has tightened considerably from the prior quarter's 15,254-job net loss.

The improvement is notable. Indiana entered Q3 with significant momentum in the wrong direction — second quarter losses had been running hot, driven by elevated closures and contracting establishments. The rebound suggests either that the worst of the mid-year adjustment is behind Indiana employers, or that the labor market has simply stopped bleeding without yet finding a new gear for growth.

National: 5.7% — Indiana outperforms

One of the more significant data points in the report is how Indiana compares to the national baseline. While the U.S. private sector recorded gross job losses equal to 5.7% of employment in Q3 2025, Indiana's figure came in at 5.1% — a 60-basis-point advantage that has been consistent across several quarters. Indiana employers are contracting and closing at a meaningfully lower rate than their national peers, even as gains remain similarly subdued.

Professional Services Carries the Quarter

Of the eleven published industry sectors in Indiana, seven recorded net job losses in Q3. The standout performer was professional and business services, which posted a net gain of 4,473 jobs — the largest sectoral gain in the state. Gross job gains in the sector reached 29,571, or 8.3% of sector employment, the highest gain rate of any sector and a jump from the prior quarter's 7.1%. Construction also held up, adding a net 1,549 jobs as the sector continues to benefit from ongoing infrastructure and residential activity.

Manufacturing Continues Its Slow Bleed

Indiana's manufacturing sector — the one most closely watched by dealmakers and regional economists — recorded a net loss of 1,291 jobs in Q3, extending a pattern of negative net employment that has defined the sector across most of the last two years. Contracting establishments lost 12,834 jobs while closing establishments shed another 1,636. The sector's gross job gain rate of 2.6% remains well below the statewide average of 5.1%, underscoring that expansion activity in Indiana manufacturing is running at roughly half the pace of the broader economy.

For M&A purposes, persistent manufacturing weakness has a dual effect. It puts downward pressure on valuations at distressed operators — potentially opening acquisition windows for strategic buyers — while also raising questions about growth assumptions in any leveraged deal where the industrial end-market is material to the thesis.

Leisure & Hospitality: The Biggest Drag

The sector posting the largest net job loss in Q3 was leisure and hospitality, which shed a net 3,409 jobs — the result of 24,092 gains against 27,501 losses. The loss rate of 8.8% for the sector was the highest of any industry in Indiana and notably above the prior quarter's 8.6%. For Indianapolis specifically, which has seen significant hotel and convention center investment over the past several years, a deteriorating hospitality employment picture warrants attention, particularly given the role event-driven demand plays in the city's economic calendar.

Transportation & Warehousing Reverses Its Gains

One of the more volatile sectors over the trailing year has been transportation and warehousing, which swung from a net gain of 4,775 jobs in Q4 2024 to a loss of 4,500 in Q1 2025, and has now settled at a net loss of 1,066 in Q3. The sector's choppiness reflects the broader recalibration of supply chain infrastructure that built up rapidly post-pandemic. For Indiana — which houses several major logistics corridors and distribution hubs — the moderation in warehousing employment is worth watching as a leading indicator of how national freight demand is translating to the regional labor market.

What This Means for Indiana Dealmakers

  • Professional and business services — Indiana's Q3 standout — is the same sector that anchors most of Indianapolis's M&A advisory, consulting, and financial services ecosystem. Strong employment momentum there supports deal activity and fee revenue at regional advisory firms.
  • Manufacturing's persistent net losses compress exit multiples for industrial operators and may push more family-owned businesses toward sale processes as owners lose confidence in organic recovery.
  • Indiana's job loss rate of 5.1% — meaningfully below the national 5.7% — is a genuine competitive advantage when pitching the state to PE-backed portfolio companies considering consolidation acquisitions.
  • The sharp improvement from Q2's −15,254 to Q3's −664 suggests the mid-year adjustment cycle has largely run its course. If that holds in Q4 data (due July 29, 2026), it would indicate a stabilized baseline heading into 2026 deal activity.
  • Leisure and hospitality weakness bears watching for any deals tied to Indianapolis's convention and event infrastructure — a sector that has seen notable capital deployment over the past three years.

The next Business Employment Dynamics release — covering Q4 2025 — is scheduled for July 29, 2026. Given the trajectory from Q2 to Q3, the key question is whether professional services can sustain its outsized gains and whether manufacturing finds a floor. Both answers will shape how Indiana's labor market story reads heading into the back half of the year.
