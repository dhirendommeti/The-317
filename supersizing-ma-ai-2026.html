Supersizing M&A for today's AI-driven landscape

Fewer but larger deals set the M&A trend as AI destroys the previously successful playbook and disrupts dealmakers

By Dhiren Dommeti & Ashrith Desu · July 8, 2026 · 5 min read · M&A · Markets
"The deal of the future will be faster, more transparent, and use far more data with far less friction. Ai will transform the M&A process just as surely as it is transforming the companies doing the transactions. For dealmakers, it's a whole new world."
— Lucy Stapleton, Global Deals Leader, PwC UK

Global M&A is on track to reach around $4 trillion in deal value this year, making this year the strongest for M&A since the post-pandemic surge. At first glance, it might look hopeful for the years to come, but the real story is that only a small number of megadeals are masking the broader weakness.

The headline is definitely impressive. Global deal value is expected to increase roughly 13% YoY, but PwC only projects 42,000 transactions on the year, representing a 13% decline YoY. Moreover, deals over $5 billion in value account for nearly half of global M&A value, as opposed to 26% just in 2024. This headline statistic of $4 trillion is largely made up of these megadeals, while the real volume has significantly decreased.

Why Bigger Deals keep Winning

Large corporations have many advantages that smaller buyers don't, from bigger balance sheets, to more accessible financing, and overall ease of scale. These megadeals are occurring due to these massive corporations purchasing assets that vertically integrate into their current operations. They strengthen supply chains, expand AI capabilities, and establish leadership in specific industries before competitors can.

The separation has started between companies that are capable of these massive transformations, and everyone else.

The Impact of AI and Capital Allocation

Capital is increasingly flowing towards the infrastructure powering the AI economy: data centers, power generation, electrical grids, cooling systems, and the infrastructure that support them. Sectors like software, facing uncertainty around AI disruption, have seen buyers become more selective in approaching those investments.

Headwinds Moving Forward

Despite the resurgence in headline value, challenges continue to weigh on the broader M&A market. PE firms still hold a record number of aging PortCo's waiting to exit. In addition to this, higher interest rates have continued to pressure financing costs, which builds off the current struggle of governments/corporations competing for capital as sectors including AI infrastructure, energy, and defense are accelerating.

What to Watch Going Forward

  1. Deal volumes continue to decline, although deal values have increased
  2. AI is transforming transaction execution by accelerating diligence processes, valuations, and investment analyses.
  3. Large strategic buyers continue driving activity in the market, while middle-market buyers have stayed constrained by struggles of financing costs, valuation gas, and economic uncertainty
  4. Market concentration is a concern, with transactions above $5 billion accounting for nearly half of total global deal value

The Bigger Picture

The M&A market isn't recovering, but becoming more and more concentrated due to the separation between massive corporations and middle-market buyers. That scale has become one of the industry's greatest competitive advantages, and the key driver for large corporations to continue expanding vertically within supply chains and their current operations.

The firms capable of utilizing their balance sheets the most continue setting records in deal values, while everyone else is waiting to be able to withstand the conditions of these transactions.
