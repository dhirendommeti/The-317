Resultant Buys Into the Deal Lifecycle

Indianapolis-based Resultant acquires Chicago's Liberty Advisor Group, combining data and digital transformation capabilities with PE-focused M&A advisory. It is a bet on owning more of what happens before and after a deal closes.

By Dhiren Dommeti · March 31, 2026 · 4 min read · Professional Services · M&A

Private equity firms have long split their consulting spend across two distinct buckets — the deal team that evaluates and structures transactions, and the operational consultants brought in afterward to execute value creation. Indianapolis-based Resultant is betting that the boundary between those two functions is collapsing, and it's buying its way into the seam.

Resultant announced the acquisition of Liberty Advisor Group, a Chicago-based consulting firm specializing in M&A advisory services for private equity sponsors and Fortune 500 companies. The deal is Resultant's fifth acquisition since 2019 and its most strategically significant — adding transaction advisory capability to a firm that has built its reputation on data analytics and digital transformation execution. Financial terms were not disclosed.

What Liberty Advisor Group Brings

Liberty Advisor Group built its practice advising private equity sponsors through the transaction lifecycle — from pre-deal commercial and operational due diligence through post-close integration and performance improvement. It counts a range of middle-market PE firms and large corporations among its clients, with particular depth in operational assessment and technology transformation advisory.

That profile complements Resultant's existing capabilities almost perfectly. Resultant has spent the past several years building a data analytics and digital execution practice that PE portfolio companies hire to implement the changes that deal teams identify. Liberty brings the front-end — the diligence and advisory work that happens before Resultant's team is typically engaged. Together, the combined firm can own a longer stretch of the engagement.

"Strategic fit is very important, but culture is even more important. Liberty's team approach and values aligned with how we operate."
— Greg Layok, CEO, Resultant

Why Investcorp-Backed Resultant Is Acquiring

Resultant has been on an acquisition path since Investcorp, the Bahrain-based alternative asset manager, took a stake in the firm. That backing gives Resultant the balance sheet to do deals that a purely bootstrapped consulting firm could not easily finance — and the expectation that the firm will grow into a platform rather than staying a single-practice shop.

The Liberty deal is the clearest expression yet of where that platform is headed. Management consulting for private equity is a highly competitive space, but firms that can combine transaction advisory with execution capability — particularly around data and technology — are increasingly attractive to sponsors who want a single partner rather than a series of specialists that need to be coordinated. Resultant is building toward that position, deliberately.

The Indianapolis Angle

Resultant is one of the more quietly impressive technology and consulting firms headquartered in Indianapolis. The city doesn't typically come to mind when PE sponsors think about management consulting partners — McKinsey, Bain, and a handful of boutiques dominate the deal advisory market. But Resultant's model is specifically built around the middle market, where the big-three firms are often too expensive and too slow, and where data-driven transformation capability is increasingly the differentiator.

The Liberty acquisition gives Resultant a Chicago-based team with PE relationships already established, expanding its geographic reach into the most active private equity market in the Midwest. The combined firm will add approximately 30 professionals from Liberty — a meaningful addition to a company that has grown primarily through technology-focused acquisitions.

What to Watch · Key Takeaways

  • This is Resultant's fifth acquisition in seven years — the pace is deliberate. Watch for additional deals as the firm builds toward a full-service PE advisory and execution platform.
  • Liberty's Chicago presence gives Resultant immediate access to the densest concentration of private equity activity in the Midwest — a market that Indianapolis-based firms often struggle to penetrate without local relationships.
  • Investcorp's backing is the enabling factor. Without institutional capital, a consulting firm Resultant's size would struggle to finance serial acquisitions of this type.
  • The integration risk is real — consulting firm mergers succeed or fail on culture. Resultant's CEO flagged this explicitly, which suggests management is aware of the challenge rather than glossing over it.
