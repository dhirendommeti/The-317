Byline Bancorp to Acquire Illinois State Bancorp in $87.9M Deal

Rosemont-based Byline Bancorp is buying the parent of First Nations Bank and The Bank of Bourbonnais in a cash-and-stock deal that would push the combined institution past $10.5 billion in assets and 48 branches across the greater Chicago area.

Dhiren Dommeti / October 6, 2026 / Banking

Byline Bancorp announced October 6 that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Illinois State Bancorp, the Chicago-area parent of First Nations Bank and The Bank of Bourbonnais, for approximately $87.9 million in a combination of cash and Byline stock. The deal is the latest chapter in a years-long build-out by Byline, which has grown from a single-market community bank into one of the more active acquirers in the Illinois commercial banking space.

Under the terms of the agreement, Illinois State Bancorp shareholders will receive roughly 1.4 million Byline shares plus $28.9 million in cash, working out to about $261.23 per share based on Byline's October 5 closing price. Outstanding stock options will be settled for an additional $5.1 million in cash. Both boards approved the deal unanimously.

Illinois State Bancorp had $617.3 million in total assets as of June 30, 2026, with $470.5 million in loans and $510.5 million in deposits. Its two subsidiary banks, First Nations Bank and The Bank of Bourbonnais, operate primarily in the south and southwest suburbs of Chicago, a market that Byline has been systematically working to deepen through acquisitions and organic branch expansion.

The combined institution would carry approximately $10.5 billion in assets, $8.0 billion in loans, and $8.4 billion in deposits, spread across 48 branches in greater Illinois. That scale puts Byline squarely in the tier of regional banks capable of competing for mid-sized commercial credits and treasury management relationships that smaller community banks often have to walk away from.

The Math Behind the Deal

Byline's investor presentation shows a price-to-tangible-book multiple of 1.07x on the target, with a last-twelve-months earnings multiple of 13.1x. Those are not aggressive numbers by Illinois community bank acquisition standards, which have seen some deals clear 1.3x to 1.5x tangible book in recent years when deposit franchises in growing suburban markets were involved. The modest premium reflects both the current interest rate environment and the fact that Illinois State Bancorp's loan book, while healthy, runs a fairly conventional residential and small business mix without the higher-yielding specialty credits that command richer multiples.

For Byline, the deal is structured to be nearly neutral to tangible book value per share, with projected dilution of just 1.2% and an earnback period of less than 18 months. That kind of discipline has become a calling card for the Rosemont-based bank, which has now completed several acquisitions without materially impairing its capital position.

First Nations Bank and the Bourbonnais Market

The Bank of Bourbonnais, which operates in Kankakee County south of Chicago, is a particularly interesting piece of this transaction. Bourbonnais has seen steady population and commercial growth tied to its location along Interstate 57 and its proximity to Illinois State University in Normal, Bradley University in Kankakee, and a significant logistics and light manufacturing corridor. Owning a deposit franchise there gives Byline a foothold in a market that does not get a lot of attention from Chicago-area banks but has been quietly growing its small business base for a decade.

First Nations Bank's footprint covers the immediate south Chicago suburbs, where Byline already has some presence. The overlap creates the kind of branch rationalization opportunity that typically surfaces modest cost savings in deals of this type, though neither party has disclosed specific efficiency targets publicly.

Illinois Banking at Scale

Byline Bancorp was founded as North Shore Community Bancorp in 2006 and went public in 2017 under the Byline name. Since then, it has executed a series of acquisitions that have taken it from a single north suburban Chicago market to a statewide commercial banking platform. Its deposit base skews toward small and mid-sized business customers, which typically carry lower deposit costs than retail-heavy institutions and provide more cross-sell opportunities in treasury and lending.

The Illinois State Bancorp acquisition is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals from relevant banking authorities and a shareholder vote by Illinois State Bancorp holders. The deal requires no Byline shareholder vote given its size relative to Byline's total assets.

The acquisition is subject to standard regulatory review, including approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and applicable state banking regulators. Byline filed the required Form 8-K with the SEC on October 6, which includes the full merger agreement and the investor presentation outlining deal terms and financial projections.

For Illinois State Bancorp shareholders, the cash-and-stock structure offers a partial liquidity event while giving them ongoing exposure to the combined franchise through Byline shares, which trade on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker BY.
