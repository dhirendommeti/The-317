Gareth Bale's Next Avenue: Sports Ownership

The former Real Madrid star launches an investment fund alongside Juggernaut Capital, highlighting the growing role of athletes in PE-backed sports investments

By Ashrith Desu · July 8, 2026 · 4 min read · Finance · Sports

Former Wales and Real Madrid player Gareth Bale has shifted over to the boardroom. Bale has partnered with Juggernaut Capital, a U.S.-based investment firm, to launch a new sports-focused investment fund, with a focus on stakes in men's and women's sports franchises and leagues.

Gale established that Cardiff City, his hometown club, does remain a target long-term, but still emphasized the broadness of the fund.

Athletes are no longer just investing their earnings, but actually approaching industries that weren't previously pursued.

What Bale Actually Built

While it looks like any new venture surface level, the investment platform was specifically designed to identify sports assets with long-term potential, similar to our previous article on Blue Owl's stake in the Cleveland Cavaliers you can find here.

The fund will evaluate men's teams, women's sports franchises/leagues, and other strategic investments across sports. While Bale did state his interest in his hometown club, future acquisitions will be driven by broader investment fundamentals, opposed to Bale's personal attachments (eg. Cardiff City)

"You have to look at it with a business brain, not just as a vanity project. So it's something that's just got to fit with everything. We're in no rush and we want to find the right club and the right team to grow that club and include the fans and the community and make it a real success."
— Gareth Bale

Their strategy? Find the right asset, pay the right price, and create long-term value.

Why Is This Important Today?

Professional athletes are increasingly partnering with institutional investors to acquire franchises. These assets have appreciated immensely over the past twenty years, driven by media rights, engagement, recurring revenue streams, and more.

While PE firms might already have the power to pursue these acquisitions, bringing in athletes gives them three major advantages: brand recognition, industry relationships, and credibility.

This combination reinforces their current expertise and builds an incredible partnership.

Under the Radar

The most interesting part of the announcement is the investment structure. In the past, athletes either bought minority stakes, or joined as passive investors. However, they've started to fully create investment platforms alongside institutional investors. With the shift to viewing sports teams as strong private assets, with the same fundamentals PE firms look for in any investment, it's become so much more prevalent.
