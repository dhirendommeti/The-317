First Financial Crosses State Lines

Terre Haute's First Financial Corp. acquires Hickory Point Bank in a $111.3 million deal, extending its reach into central Illinois and signaling continued consolidation among Indiana's community banks.

By The 317 Staff · August 26, 2026 · 4 min read · Banking · M&A

Community banking consolidation in the Midwest rarely makes national headlines, but this deal says something about where the region's smaller banks are headed. First Financial Corporation, headquartered in Terre Haute, Indiana, has agreed to acquire First Illinois Corporation — parent of Hickory Point Bank and Trust — in a transaction valued at approximately $111.3 million. It's the latest sign that Indiana's community banks, facing rising compliance costs and slimmer margins, are looking across the Wabash for scale.

What First Financial Is Buying

Hickory Point Bank and Trust operates eight branches concentrated in central Illinois — Decatur, Springfield, and Champaign — markets that First Financial has not previously served. As of June 30, 2026, the bank held approximately $717 million in total assets, $438 million in total loans, and $627 million in total deposits. The transaction is structured so that 70% of First Illinois common stock is exchanged for First Financial shares, with the remaining 30% paid in cash at an implied price of $45.00 per share.

The deal gives First Financial an immediate footprint in three of Illinois' larger secondary cities, each anchored by state government employment, a university, or both. Decatur hosts several large agricultural processing operations. Champaign-Urbana carries the University of Illinois. Springfield is the state capital. For a community bank looking to diversify its loan book, these markets offer a different economic profile than western Indiana.

Why the Timing Makes Sense

The pressure on smaller community banks has been building for years. Regulatory compliance costs that were once manageable for a $500 million institution have grown substantially. Technology investment — core banking upgrades, digital banking platforms, fraud detection — requires scale to justify. And net interest margins, while recovering from their post-pandemic compression, remain thin enough that efficiency ratios matter more than ever.

For First Illinois, the deal offers shareholders a clean exit at a premium to book value. For First Financial, it adds low-cost deposit funding, a new geographic market, and enough scale to spread fixed costs across a larger balance sheet. The combined organization will hold approximately $6.9 billion in total assets — still community-sized, but well past the threshold where operational leverage starts to meaningfully improve.

"This transaction advances our long-term growth strategy and gives us an outstanding partner in First Illinois."
— First Financial Corporation management, per SEC filing, August 2026

The Broader Pattern

This deal fits a pattern that has been playing out across the Midwest for the better part of a decade. Indiana's community banking landscape has compressed steadily — the number of state-chartered institutions has declined as mergers outpace new charters. The banks that remain independent tend to be either very local and relationship-driven, or actively acquiring to build the scale needed to compete.

First Financial has been in the latter camp. The Hickory Point acquisition is not its first move across a state line, and it likely won't be its last. Central Illinois, in particular, has a high density of community banks relative to population — which means more potential sellers for a buyer with the currency and appetite to keep growing.

What to Watch · Key Takeaways

  • The deal is structured 70% stock / 30% cash at $45.00 per First Illinois share — shareholders who want liquidity can take the cash election; those who want continued upside can take stock in the combined entity.
  • Regulatory approval is the key gating item — the deal is expected to close in Q4 2026, which assumes no extended review from Illinois or Indiana regulators.
  • The Champaign-Urbana market, anchored by the University of Illinois, adds a deposit base that tends to be stickier than purely commercial banking markets — a strategic plus for First Financial's funding profile.
  • Watch for additional M&A from First Financial. At $6.9 billion pro forma, the bank is still well below the $10 billion asset threshold where regulatory scrutiny intensifies significantly — leaving room to keep acquiring.
