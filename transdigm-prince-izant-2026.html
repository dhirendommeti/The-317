TransDigm's New Specialization in Aerospace Power

The aerospace giant is acquiring Prince & Izant, adding a specialized supplier with proprietary components, serving applications across aerospace and defense.

TransDigm Group announced it will acquire Cleveland-based Prince & Izant from Industrial Growth Partners for just over $1 billion in cash. P&I is expected to generate approximately $360 million in revenue from the deal, displaying a 3.0x revenue multiple for the business. Beyond revenue, financial details were not disclosed.

Prince & Izant designs and manufactured specialty metal components used in applications where performance failures can be extremely costly. These parts serve sectors across aerospace and defense, transportation, medical, industrial markets, and aeroderivative turbines.

Within aerospace and defense, these products are applied in engine fuel nozzles and rocket engines. Additionally, the company manufactures within Ohio, Illinois, Wisconsin, and New York, along with its product catalog spanning almost 10,000 active SKUs (Stock Keeping Unit).

For TransDigm this is particularly significant, as the majority of Prince & Izant's revenue comes from aftermarket sales, meaning its products support an installed base of equipment already in service. Once a specialized component is installed on an aircraft, replacing it is not necessarily a simple decision. Customers require products that meet precise specifications, which is exactly how Prince & Izant builds. The products they create can be extremely difficult to replicate or substitute.

Behind The Scenes

Prince & Izant doesn't simply sell commodity metals. Their revenue mainly comes from alloys (gold, silver, platinum), but their expertise is what makes them valuable. Their ability to turn those materials into high performance components in high-consequence applications is why their specialization in the sector is respected.

"The company offers highly engineered, custom, proprietary products and provides excellent service to its customers – attributes that align well with TransDigm's acquisition criteria." — Mike Lisman, CEO, TransDigm

That's the actual niche TransDigm is gaining. They described P&I's products as highly proprietary and tied to extreme applications, specifically the type of component where customers value and respect a company on their reliability and performance rather than price or convenience.
